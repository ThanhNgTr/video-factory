# ADR 0016: Tight speech, caption beats, and the length budget that follows

Date: 2026-11-17 · Status: accepted

## Context
A second working session fixed three things on episode 1 of night-diner without an ADR: edge-tts leaves about 1 s of silence after every sentence and 0.9 s at the end of a clip (a quarter of an episode), "hon" was spoken as "Han", and a caption chunk of one word flashed for a few frames. Reviewing those changes found that the caption fix also pre-revealed punch words, that episode 1 had been re-voiced but not re-rendered, and that every length estimate in the repo was calibrated on the old, slower speech.

## Decision
- **Tight speech** (`modules/audio/src/domain/silence.ts`, `EdgeTts`): ffmpeg `silencedetect` finds the pauses; each inner pause is cut to 0.4 s, the lead and tail to 0.15 s, the clip is rebuilt with `atrim`/`concat`, and the word timings shift by the silence removed before them. The TTS cache keeps the raw clip. `normalizeSpeech` reads "hon" as "hun". `audio.tts` version 2. Episode 1 went from 125 s to 95.5 s with the same script.
- **Caption beats** (`captionChunks`): a one-word chunk joins the chunk before it when that chunk was cut mid-phrase by the three-word limit ("say your | name."), or ended on a comma and the word follows within 10 frames ("coming, | hon."). It never joins after a full stop or after a comma and a real pause: "first shift. | Again." stays two beats, otherwise the punch word shows (greyed) a second before it is spoken.
- **Length budget recalibrated:** the narrator now runs about 10 characters per second plus about 7 s of pauses and cards (885 characters, 95.5 s). The series format keeps 1000 spoken characters (about 1:45, inside its 60-130 s range; shorter holds viewers better) and its writing rule now says so. The Studio's editor meter takes the rate from one place (`SPEECH` in `apps/cli/src/studio/model.ts`), handed to the page script as data attributes.
- With the same review, two framing fixes the frame reviewer had reported: medium and close shots of a character holding an item out shift toward the hand (`reach`, sharing the hand position with insert shots), and the headlights sweep follows the set's windows instead of assuming the +x wall. `video.render` version 4.
- **Framing from the re-render's frame review** (`video.render` version 5), all four defects the reviewer found on episode 1:
  - Room shots fit each person at their own depth (`fitRoom`): the old fit used the group's average depth, so whoever stood nearest the lens was cut by the frame edge, and a push was framed for its start, not its end. Backed against the front wall, the camera also slides toward the nearest person, which needs the least widening of the lens.
  - Close shots (closeup, medium, over-shoulder, insert) leave out floor props on the camera's side that are more than 1 m from the person framed (`foreground`), as a film crew moves a stool out of frame. The person's own counter or stool stays.
  - A shot with a title, rule card or the vote card tilts the camera up by about 9% of the frame so faces sit under the text, and the vote card is compact enough to end at about a third of the height.
- **Leaving through a door** (`exitPoint`, `video.render` version 6): room shots show everyone present, so a walker missing from the next room shot has left. After reaching their spot they walk on to the nearest door and past its wall, which hides them, and the camera keeps the door in frame. The roadside diner gets the kitchen door its far-corner spot already named; without it, Dot walked into an empty corner and was simply gone from the next shots.
- **A stage can fingerprint the data it reads besides its artifacts** (`StageDef.fingerprint`, part of the cache key only when present). `assets.check` fingerprints the library's people, actions and sets: before, a library edit never reached an existing run's render, because the stage was cached on `scene.json` alone. LLM stages deliberately have none, so a library edit does not re-stage finished episodes.
- **The budget caps one invocation, not a run's lifetime** (`runStages`): `VF_BUDGET_USD` (optional since 2026-11-17: unset means no cap; it was a $2 default the producer never chose) is checked against the cost rows written since the invocation started, marked by row id, not by time, since two invocations can land in the same millisecond. Re-staging and re-rendering episode 1 a dozen times had added up to $2 and blocked a $0.30 re-run; `runCost` still reports the lifetime total.

## Consequences
- A changed TTS pause rule changes every episode's length and timeline: re-run from `audio.tts` and re-render. Two sessions editing the same run left episode 1's video out of step with its audio until it was re-rendered; work one session at a time, or commit before switching.
- The speech rate is fitted on one episode; refit `SPEECH` and the format text after a few more.
